Section 01

Document Control, Scope & Methodology

Assessment Objective

Northwind Holding completed its Defender for Endpoint rollout 14 months ago. Since then the estate has grown by roughly 700 devices and two subsidiaries have been absorbed. This health check validates the deployment against Microsoft best practice and the client’s internal endpoint standard, quantifies the residual gaps, and produces a prioritised remediation roadmap that the endpoint and SOC teams can execute within one budget year.

  • Telemetry analysis — 26 KQL queries across a 90-day telemetry window (DeviceInfo, DeviceEvents, DeviceTvmSoftwareVulnerabilities, AlertInfo)
  • Interviews / workshops — 4 workshops: SOC tier 1/2, endpoint engineering, IT operations, identity team

Section 02

Executive Summary

We assessed the Microsoft Defender for Endpoint deployment across 3,322 in-scope devices. Protection fundamentals are sound — antivirus, cloud protection and EDR telemetry are working well on the Windows estate — but the deployment is not finished: 308 devices are unprotected, and 11 of the 20 attack surface reduction rules still run in audit mode, so they observe attacks without stopping them. Completing onboarding and moving ASR to block mode are the two actions that will reduce risk fastest.

Windows clients are well covered (97% onboarded) and centrally managed through Intune. Servers lag at 88%, and the legacy Windows Server 2012 R2 population is effectively unprotected. macOS and Linux were onboarded as a pilot and never completed; mobile devices are enrolled in Intune but not onboarded to Defender at all. The SOC triages alerts in the Defender portal during business hours with an MSSP covering nights and weekends; automated investigation is enabled at semi-automatic level for most device groups.

The unprotected populations sit in the same network zones as finance and HR systems. A commodity ransomware intrusion on one of the 34 unprotected 2012 R2 servers would be neither prevented nor visible to the SOC until it reached a monitored host — historically a 6- to 18-hour blind window, long enough for domain-wide encryption. The audit-mode ASR rules recorded 14,820 events in 30 days that would have been blocked in enforcement mode.

Top Findings at a Glance

  1. 308 in-scope devices have no Defender sensor; 34 of them are internet-reachable servers
  2. 11 of 20 ASR rules remain in audit mode, including LSASS credential theft protection
  3. Tamper protection is enabled on only 78% of devices and is not enforced tenant-wide
  4. Path exclusions include three whole-drive and user-writable wildcard entries
  5. Device risk signals are not used in Conditional Access; compromised devices retain access

Strengths Observed

  • Cloud-delivered protection at advanced level with a 99.1% real-time block rate on the Windows estate
  • Intune-based policy management is consistent; no conflicting GPO/Intune AV policies were found on managed clients
  • The SOC has written 17 custom detection rules mapped to MITRE ATT&CK and reviews them quarterly
